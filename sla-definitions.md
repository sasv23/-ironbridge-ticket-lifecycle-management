# Ironbridge Bank & Trust | SLA Definitions

## SLA Goals

| Priority | Time to first response | Time to resolution |
|----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| **Critical** | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| **High** | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| **Medium** | 4 hours | 2 business days |
| **Low** | 1 business day | 5 business days |

## Which goals did I put on business hours, and which on 24/7?

I put Critical for both “Time to resolution & Time to first response.” Critical can mean it can affect major systems and, even if after hours, needs to be addressed.

## A branch issue at 8 PM is not urgent, but a wire system issue at 8 PM might be. Why?

A branch issue at 8 pm means the branches are closed with no customers, but a wire system issue at 8 is critical. It needs to be ready for the next working day and is essential.

## What pause condition did I set, and when does the clock stop?

For the status “Waiting for customer,” because once the work is done and waiting for the customer, time for the SLA still ticks.

## What is the difference between a response goal and a resolution goal, and why does response matter so much to the person waiting?

A response goal is how fast the service desk first answers a ticket, and a resolution goal is how fast the problem is actually solved and closed. Response matters most to the person waiting because it tells them someone has their ticket and they haven’t been forgotten. Silence is what frustrates people, even more than a slow fix, as Charlene found when her scanner report sat unanswered for three weeks.
