# Ironbridge Bank & Trust | IT Priority Matrix

Priority is impact combined with urgency. This matrix is written for Ironbridge Bank & Trust, a 160 employee community bank with six branches, a wire transfer operation, and a lending division.

## Priority Levels

| Priority | What it means at a bank | Example |
|----------|-------------------------|---------|
| **Critical** | A core banking function is unavailable or a branch cannot serve customers. | Wire system unavailable at 3 PM |
| **High** | A major function is degraded, or a group of people is affected, but the branch can still serve customers. Work is being delayed or done the hard way every day, and needs to be fixed today. | A teller is locked out at 9:01 AM with customers in the lobby |
| **Medium** | Something is not working properly, but a workaround is available. People can keep working, but it is slower and less convenient. Needs to be fixed but can wait. | The CFO cannot open a spreadsheet |
| **Low** | A minor issue or routine request with no effect on customers or daily operations. No work is blocked. | — |

## Worked Examples

### 1. A teller is locked out at 9:01 AM with customers in the lobby

- **Who is affected:** Customers are the first affected people, but also the branch as a whole, including one teller.
- **Is damage accumulating right now:** Yes, every second the issue is not resolved, damage is accumulating. Customers leave, and other tellers need to take in the extra work.
- **Priority I would set:** High. Customers are in the lobby and every minute the issue stays open, more of them leave and the other tellers take on extra work. The branch is still open and serving people, which keeps this below Critical. It becomes Critical only if this is the only teller and no one can be served at all.

### 2. The CFO cannot open a spreadsheet

- **Who is affected and is damage accumulating:** Just CFO, he is able to work on other assignments. He can also use a backed up copy while issue is being looked at.
- **Priority I would set, and why his title does not change the answer:** Medium, priority is based on impact to system, not title of person. Whether it is the CFO or a general employee are the same when it comes to the issue.

### 3. The wire system is unavailable at 3 PM

- **Who is affected and is damage accumulating:** Wire operations staff, plus every customer with a transfer pending today. Yes, damage is building right now. It's already 3 PM, so the longer the system is down, the more likely wires miss the daily cutoff and get delayed to the next business day. That means late payments for customers and a bad look for the bank.
- **Priority I would set:** Critical, it is an essential banking function, while unavailable with deadline coming up.

### 4. The Elm Street check scanner fails intermittently, and has for three weeks

- **Who is affected and what workaround are they living with:** The staff of that bank are affected, as well as the customers. The workaround they are living with is potentially sending an employee to another branch or bank office and inputting the information manually.
- **Is damage accumulating right now:** Yes, but slowly. With the workarounds happening, more time is being taken away, as well as manual entries allow potential errors. 3 weeks of this will result in damage accumulating.
- **Priority I would set:** Medium priority because there is a workaround in place. 3 weeks of this means the original cause has not been resolved.
